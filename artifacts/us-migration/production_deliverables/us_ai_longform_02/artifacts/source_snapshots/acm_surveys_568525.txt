A Survey of Rollback-Recovery Protocols in Message-Passing Systems
Authors: E. N. (Mootaz) Elnozahy, Lorenzo Alvisi, Yi-Min Wang, David B. Johnson
Journal: ACM Computing Surveys (CSUR), Vol. 34, No. 3, September 2002, pp. 375–408
DOI: 10.1145/568522.568525
Canonical URL: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/568522.568525

Abstract:
Rollback-recovery techniques solve the problem of making distributed systems fault-tolerant without user intervention. By saving process state periodically during normal execution, a system can restore a consistent global state following a transient failure or crash. This survey provides a comprehensive taxonomy of checkpointing and message logging protocols.

Key Theoretical and Architecture Foundations:
Coordinated checkpointing protocols record consistent process states to stable storage, ensuring that upon a crash or unhandled failure, the system rolls back to the most recent consistent state, eliminating cascading rollbacks and bounded recovery latency. In multi-stage processing systems, persisting immutable checkpoints at pipeline stage boundaries guarantees fault containment and deterministic replay.
